New York Times Sues Brave Over AI Scraping and Paywall Bypass

The New York Times has filed a lawsuit against Brave browser, alleging it scraped copyrighted content to train AI models and allowed users to bypass paywalls without consent.

The Verge AI · Nov 02, 2026

What happened

  • The New York Times and other publications have sued Brave for alleged unlawful scraping of content to train AI.
  • Brave has allegedly provided 'stolen copyrighted content' to multiple AI companies.
  • Brave's Paywall blocker feature allows users to bypass publishers' paywalls without consent.

Why it matters

This legal action highlights the tension between AI development and copyright law, as major publications seek to hold tech companies accountable for unauthorized data use. It raises questions about the ethics of AI training on scraped content and the potential for legal precedents in digital rights.

The Elephant take

🐘 鼋 The lawsuit is a sharp reminder that even with 'ethical' AI, the line between innovation and infringement can blur. Brave's actions may be seen as a 'back door' to the AI industry, but the legal battle shows how fragile the balance of power is in the digital space.

Who should care

  • Publishers
  • AI developers
  • Legal professionals
  • Tech companies

What to do next

  1. Monitor legal developments in AI copyright cases
  2. Review terms of service for browser and AI platforms
  3. Assess potential risks to content integrity
  4. Consult with legal experts on data usage policies

Keep in mind

The case hinges on legal definitions of 'stolen content' and 'unauthorized access', which could shift based on future rulings.
