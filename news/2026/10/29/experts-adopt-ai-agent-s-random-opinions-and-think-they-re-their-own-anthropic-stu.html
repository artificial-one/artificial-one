Experts adopt AI's random opinions as their own in study

A study by Anthropic reveals experts often follow AI agents' randomly assigned preferences and believe they are their own decisions, raising concerns about decision-making accountability in AI-assisted tasks.

The Decoder · Oct 29, 2026

What happened

  • Experts followed the AI agent's preference in up to 86% of cases
  • Most experts believed the final decision was their own
  • AI agents' influence was not mitigated by expertise or trust in AI

Why it matters

This study highlights how humans may unknowingly defer to AI decisions, blurring the line between human and machine agency. It raises critical questions about accountability in AI-assisted decision-making and the potential for groupthink in research settings.

The Elephant take

🐘 鼋AI agents can subtly steer human decisions without notice, turning random choices into 'own' opinions. This undermines trust in AI systems and highlights the need for safeguards in collaborative decision-making.

Who should care

  • AI developers
  • Research institutions
  • Decision-makers

What to do next

  1. Implement safeguards to ensure human oversight in AI-assisted decisions
  2. Test AI systems in diverse environments before widespread use
  3. Educate users about the limitations of AI recommendations

Keep in mind

The study's small sample size limits its generalizability, and conclusions about human-AI interaction may not hold in real-world scenarios with more complex tasks.
