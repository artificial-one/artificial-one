AI access reduces willingness to admit uncertainty

A study shows that having access to AI answers significantly decreases people's willingness to say 'I don't know', even when the AI is often wrong. Participants became more confident but less accurate when using AI advice.

The Decoder · Sep 26, 2026

What happened

  • AI access drastically reduces willingness to admit uncertainty
  • Participants became more confident but less accurate with AI advice
  • Financial incentives slightly mitigate the effect of AI access

Why it matters

The study highlights a concerning trend where reliance on AI diminishes human judgment. As AI becomes more prevalent, people may become overly confident in uncertain situations, leading to potential errors in decision-making.

The Elephant take

🐘 🦘 The study reveals a paradox: AI makes people more certain but less accurate. It's a warning about the dangers of over-relying on technology for answers we're not fully sure about.

Who should care

  • Researchers
  • Tech companies
  • Educators

What to do next

  1. Question AI's reliability before relying on it
  2. Encourage critical thinking in uncertain situations
  3. Test AI answers against multiple sources

Keep in mind

The findings are based on controlled experiments with specific types of questions, so real-world implications may vary.
